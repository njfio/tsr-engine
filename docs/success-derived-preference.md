## Path and boundaries

1. Ordinary returned execution evidence reaches the existing source-bound
   criterion assessor. An accepted assessment with all published artifact findings
   met and an explicit partitioned scope records a Council `ScopedSuccess`.
2. The model may retain, abstain, act normally, or propose a relationship. An
   independently admitted aspiration may then be proposed as a new identity using
   `success_preference` with request-local aspiration/success references. The
   aspiration supplies its rationale; the reference-only envelope adds no prose.
3. A separate runtime review assesses the connection to the contributors and
   authentic scoped success. Native preview alone cannot admit it. The explicit
   preference advance atomically records Generator `SuccessExpanded`, a truthful
   Council review attestation, and `ReviewedSuccessPreferenceAdmission`.
4. The normal ledger contains the normal content-addressed `Preference`, Open
   after Council admission, with explicit `from_success` provenance and original
   parents. It participates in generic selection and ordinary composition, and
   can contribute alongside Contrast-derived identities. Restart reconstructs it
   from the log; there is no parallel notebook identity.

The original criteria, parent identities, history, permissions and action scope
are unchanged. New identity admission does not fulfill any parent or successor.
Human fulfillment and user acceptance remain unknown. Stop withdraws operational
permission, not contributing history. No new wanting is required after success.

The generic ledger verifies causal ordering and trusts the Council controller
attestation; it cannot independently authenticate semantic judgment from arbitrary
Council records. The project verifies the exact current criterion assessment,
receipt, publication, source, criteria and scope. The transport verifies ownership
of the assessment's retained request/response or an explicitly pinned import.
Neither structural validation nor a fallible independent model review proves
that a proposed desire is philosophically meaningful.

Initial scope is conservative: at least two distinct original ancestry roots are
required by the existing composition bridge, and every published artifact finding
must be met. These are semantic scope policies, not universal TSR requirements
or unavoidable evidence-integrity safeguards.
Partial success within a mixed reject/unknown assessment is not a success basis
in this version. The existing unmet-Contrast route remains unchanged. Legacy
assessments/project-only nodes are not migrated or retroactively promoted.

## Follow-up scope audit

The two-root rule predates these commits in `project/expansion.rs`. It reuses the
relational composition contract (at least two distinct active direct contributors)
and checks ancestry against the original acceptance mappings. It does **not**
require root identities to be repeated as direct contributors. A reviewed native
child retains its direct parents and flattened original contributors; an eligible
child, including a Fulfilled child, can participate in another reviewed composition.
Same-identity reviewed development keeps its identity and ancestry. Original
acceptance mappings anchor evidence coverage; they do not replace the child's
meaning or permission boundary. The strengthened mixed-lineage test deliberately
omits one root from direct parents and reaches it only through the child.

The all-met policy is new to this success path (`project/success.rs` and
`project/criterion_return.rs`). It allows an unambiguous complete published
artifact-success attestation and avoids silently selecting a favorable subset.
It is stricter than the user's general permission for successful fulfillment to
catalyze new wanting. Authentic subset success could be represented safely with
explicit subset scope and review; the current implementation does not do that.
An unrelated unknown/unmet finding therefore prevents this path even if some
contributing criteria succeeded. This is a disclosed capability limit, not proof
that partial success cannot produce wanting. No subset policy was silently added
or relaxed in this audit. The existing Contrast route remains separate.

Necessary safeguards are exact current source/receipt/criteria scope, authenticated
Council assessment and separate semantic admission, real native identity, retained
ancestry, known eligible contributors, unchanged obligations/permissions, and
transaction/restart/Stop validation. Neither root count nor whole-assessment
success alone establishes philosophical culmination or human fulfillment.

Independent follow-up review found no recursive-participation blocker and agreed
with this distinction between semantic scope policies and integrity safeguards.

The inherited two-root condition also excludes a new identity arising solely from
one root, even if several descendants of that root interact. Existing same-identity
development remains available. This is another scope boundary of the reused
composition bridge, not an evidence theorem; this follow-up does not redefine it.
